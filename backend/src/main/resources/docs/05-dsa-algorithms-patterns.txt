Vicky Assist FAANG Training — Behavioral Interviews and STAR Stories

Why behavioral rounds matter
Top companies reject strong coders for weak ownership, collaboration, or leadership signals.

STAR method
- Situation: brief context
- Task: your responsibility
- Action: what YOU did (most important)
- Result: measurable outcome + lesson

Story bank themes to prepare (8–12 stories)
1. Leadership without authority
2. Conflict with a teammate or manager
3. Failure / mistake and recovery
4. Tight deadline delivery
5. Ambiguous requirements
6. Mentoring or unblocking others
7. Disagree and commit
8. Customer obsession / user impact
9. Diving deep into a production issue
10. Invent and simplify (reduce complexity)

Amazon Leadership Principles (high-yield themes)
- Customer Obsession
- Ownership
- Dive Deep
- Bias for Action
- Earn Trust
- Deliver Results
- Invent and Simplify
- Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
Map each story to 1–2 principles.

Answer tips
- Keep Situation short (2–3 sentences)
- Use "I" more than "we" for your actions
- Include numbers: latency -40%, errors -70%, revenue +X, time saved
- End with learning applied later
- Prepare follow-ups: "What would you do differently?"

Sample conflict skeleton
Situation: Frontend and backend disagreed on API contract before launch.
Task: Align teams without delaying release.
Action: Facilitated a short design review, proposed versioned endpoint, wrote migration plan, got sign-off.
Result: Shipped on time; reduced integration bugs; later reused the versioning checklist.

Sample failure skeleton
Situation: Deployed a config change that raised error rates.
Task: Restore service and prevent recurrence.
Action: Rolled back, added canary + config validation, wrote postmortem.
Result: MTTR under 20 minutes; similar incidents did not recur next quarter.

Questions to ask interviewers
- How is success measured in the first 6 months?
- What does the on-call / quality culture look like?
- How are design reviews run on this team?
- What are the biggest technical challenges now?

Anti-patterns
- Blaming others without ownership
- Vague stories with no result
- Memorized buzzwords with no specifics
- Speaking only about team wins with no personal actions
